The last step, and the only one that finishes setup — whether you imported a file
or typed everything in.

## What BitGigs needs

Two things, each shown with a tick or a cross:

- **Tax details** — one tax profile.
- **A workplace and its pay terms** — a job, and how it pays.

That's the whole requirement. Anything missing gets a **Fill this in** button
that jumps to the right step, and **Finish setup** stays disabled until both are
ticked. Each line says where it came from: *imported from your file*, or *will be
created when you finish*.

**Shifts never block anything.** If your file brought some they're listed, but
only so you can see they arrived — setup finishes perfectly well with zero.

## Workplaces that still need pay terms

If your import file logged shifts against a workplace it never actually
described, BitGigs creates that workplace so the shifts aren't lost — but it has
no pay terms yet, so those shifts can't be priced.

Review names any such workplace and **Finish stays disabled** until it's filled
in. The button takes you to the pay terms step with a blank form for it; if
several are waiting, you'll be sent back for the next one.

## Filling a gap

Two ways, and you can mix them:

- **Import another file** — useful when your data is split across exports. Later
  imports see what earlier ones created, so a renamed workplace can be mapped
  onto an existing one.
- **Fill this in** — go to the step and type it.

## Your account

Review also shows the account you created in step 1. **Edit** and **Change** open
the same dialogs the settings page uses, so you can fix a typo in your email or
change your password without leaving setup.

## Starting over

**Start over** clears everything setup has gathered — imported workplaces, pay
terms and shifts, your tax details, and anything typed into the steps — and
returns you to [Start](/help/onboarding-start/). It asks for confirmation first,
and it leaves your account alone: you stay signed in and won't need the setup key
again. Handy if you imported the wrong file.

## Finish

**Finish setup** writes anything still held as a draft — all at once, so a failure
can't leave half a setup behind — and opens your dashboard.
